# LinkedIn post format

## Shape

LinkedIn posts in this corpus run roughly 400-650 words (4-7 paragraphs). They're tighter and more
declarative than the newsletters — no greeting, no sign-off name, written to work as a standalone
scroll-stopper with no subject line to set context.

1. **Hook (1-3 sentences).** A specific, checkable claim, often about something the reader personally
   owns or has seen ("The niacinamide serum you're using probably..."), or a scene ("Last September I
   was at a trade fair in Mumbai...").
2. **Framing sentence.** One line establishing why this is worth reading: "I want to explain why,
   because I've seen this misunderstood enough times..." Sets up that what follows is a real
   explanation, not a listicle.
3. **The deconstruction.** The technical body — usually built around a small number of discrete
   factors, introduced with plain sequencing language ("The second thing...", "The third thing...")
   rather than bullet points or numbered lists. No markdown formatting, no bold, no bullets — LinkedIn
   posts in this voice are prose, not formatted content.
4. **A concrete example**, ideally with real numbers — either a general industry example or a
   Skinstinct-specific one.
5. **The honesty turn** — a short paragraph explicitly bounding the claim ("I am not making a case
   that niacinamide doesn't work...").
6. **Closer: what to do about it**, addressed directly to the reader, phrased as a question they should
   ask or a thing they should check — not a product pitch. The Founder Story post (linkedin_002) closes
   differently — reflectively, on the founding rationale itself — because it's the one post that's
   inherently a narrative rather than an explainer; match that tonal exception only when writing a
   founder-story post.

## Category taxonomy (from the four seed posts)

Use one of these framings, or a clear variant of one, when asked for a LinkedIn post without more
specific direction:

- **Ingredient Deep-Dive** — take one ingredient/active, show that the label claim (a percentage, a
  named compound) is necessary but not sufficient, walk through the real formulation variables that
  determine whether it works.
- **Founder Story** — a specific real moment (not a montage of the whole company history) that reveals
  something about why Skinstinct's process is the way it is. Understated, not inspirational-poster
  in tone.
- **India-Specific Context** — a market-specific formulation or regulatory issue (climate, labelling
  law, ingredient sourcing) that gets skipped when brands import formulations or claims from other
  markets.
- **Industry Transparency** — an unregulated or loosely-regulated claim ("clinically tested,"
  "natural," "clean") and what it actually does and doesn't guarantee.

## What NOT to do in this format

- Don't add a bullet-point listicle structure ("3 things your serum label isn't telling you 👇") — this
  voice never uses bullets, numbered headers, or emoji in LinkedIn posts, even though the content is
  structured around 2-4 factors internally.
- Don't open with a question as a hook ("Ever wondered why your serum feels different in monsoon?").
  The real posts open with a statement or a scene, never an engagement-bait question.
- Don't end with a call to follow, share, or comment. None of the four seed posts do this.
- Don't compress the honesty-turn paragraph out to save space — it's structurally load-bearing, not
  filler, and cutting it is the single most common way a draft will read as generic LinkedIn content
  instead of Meera's voice.
