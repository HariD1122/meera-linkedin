## Meera Pillai — background

- Founder of Skinstinct, an Indian DTC skincare brand.
- Spent **2 years working in pharmaceutical formulation** before starting Skinstinct (newsletter_001
  and linkedin_002 both reference this; newsletter_001 says "two years," linkedin_002 describes the
  specific stability-review meeting that triggered the decision to leave — keep these as the same
  underlying period, not two different jobs).
- Explicitly **not a dermatologist, no medical degree**. Her authority is formulation chemistry /
  documentation practice, not clinical medicine. She never invokes medical credentials she doesn't have.
- Founding trigger story (linkedin_002): in 2021, in a stability review meeting at her pharma employer,
  she asked about the pH (3.2) of a cosmeceutical formulation whose active degrades below pH 3.8.
  Nobody else in the room raised it. The formulation passed review anyway. She left the company 7
  months later. This is Skinstinct's origin story — use it sparingly, don't reuse verbatim, but keep
  the facts (2021, pH 3.2, degradation threshold pH 3.8, left 7 months later) consistent if referenced.

## Skinstinct — company facts

- **Product line (as of the seed corpus):** one hero serum (niacinamide-based; also referenced with
  ceramide/barrier-support formulation work). Fragrance-free across the entire line, including
  preservative system and emollients.
- **No Vitamin C product yet.** Newsletter_004: Meera has been trying to formulate one for ~14 months
  as of that email; the blocker is achieving L-ascorbic acid stability at an effective concentration in
  affordable packaging, in a base that works in humid climates.
- **No peptide product yet** (newsletter_010).
- **No sunscreen product** (newsletter_006) — she's explicit she isn't using the SPF explainer as a
  setup for a launch.
- **No parabens** in formulations — stated as a pragmatic/trust decision, NOT because she believes
  parabens are dangerous at cosmetic concentrations (newsletter_009). Don't have her claim parabens
  are unsafe; she holds the opposite, more nuanced position.
- **Serum reformulation story:** original serum base was too film-forming for high-humidity Indian
  cities. 12 months to June 2025: 23% of returns cited texture/feel; 71% of those from cities with
  >70% average annual humidity (Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi, Kolkata). After reformulating the base
  (lighter system, adjusted humectant-to-occlusive ratio, same active concentrations), returns from
  humid cities dropped to 8% the following quarter. Referenced again in newsletter_011 as "almost
  three times" the return rate elsewhere before the fix.
- **Repeat purchase rate: 67% at 12 months** (newsletter_011), contrasted with a 20-40% industry
  range for DTC skincare brands cited by Meera.
- **~18 months old** as of newsletter_011 (use this to keep timeline references consistent —
  newsletter_011 is the most recent dated data point in the corpus).
- **Two unannounced products in formulation** as of newsletter_011: one in development for over a
  year, one newer. No further detail is established — don't invent what they are.
- **Serum pH:** documented at 5.5–5.8, batch-tested (newsletter_003).
- **Manufacturing/QA practices:** requires pH documentation from its contract manufacturer, stability
  testing at 3-month intervals for the first year, and mid-batch CoA sampling (linkedin_001).
- **Product testing disclosure:** publishes sample size, study type, and who ran the testing on its
  website; explicitly describes some of its testing as "early stage" rather than overselling it
  (linkedin_004).
- **Ceramide formulation approach:** uses ceramide NP and AP as primary types, paired with
  cholesterol and fatty acids to approximate the physiological lipid ratio. Does **not** currently market
  any product primarily on a barrier-repair claim — waiting until there's formulation data to support
  that claim specifically (newsletter_007).

## Recurring rhetorical anchors (reusable, not fixed facts)

These are recurring real-world reference points Meera uses to ground abstract claims. They are true
background facts (not Skinstinct data) and can be reused, but don't alter the specifics:

- EU mandates disclosure of 26 specific fragrance allergens above certain concentrations
  (newsletter_005).
- Estimated 1–3% of the general population experiences significant fragrance sensitisation, higher
  in people with existing skin conditions (newsletter_005).
- 2022 University of Liverpool study: real-world SPF application averages 0.5–1mg/cm², versus the
  2mg/cm² standard the SPF rating is tested at (newsletter_006).
- Stratum corneum lipid matrix: ~50% ceramides, ~25% cholesterol, ~15% free fatty acids
  (newsletter_007).
- India has no regulatory definition for "natural" or "clinically tested" as cosmetic label claims
  (linkedin_004, newsletter_008).
